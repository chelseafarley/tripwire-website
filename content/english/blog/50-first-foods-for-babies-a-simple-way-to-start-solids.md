## What are the 50 first foods?

Instead of worrying about perfect meals, focus on gradually giving your baby opportunities to experience a wide variety of foods. There isn’t one perfect order.

A varied first-food journey might include vegetables, fruit, grains, legumes, proteins, dairy, eggs, fish, nut products prepared appropriately for babies, herbs and mild spices, and different textures.

Rather than asking “What should my baby eat today?”, you can ask:

> “What new food could we explore next?”

## When can babies start solid foods?

Most babies begin exploring solid foods at **around six months**, once they are developmentally ready. Early feeding is also about taste, texture, smell, and the experience of eating — not racing through a checklist.

**50 foods is a journey, not a deadline.**

## Why introduce lots of different foods?

The first months of eating are a chance to discover sweet and savoury flavours, mild bitterness, aromas, smooth and mashed foods, soft finger foods, temperatures, and new combinations.

A baby might happily eat avocado one day and throw it on the floor the next. That’s normal. The goal of **50 First Foods** isn’t a baby who loves every food — it’s keeping opportunities to **explore variety**.

## Baby-led weaning or purées?

You don’t have to choose one philosophy. Whether avocado was mashed or an appropriate finger food, it can still become part of your baby’s food experience.

## A simple starting solids routine

1. Choose a food your baby hasn’t explored yet  
2. Prepare it appropriately  
3. Let your baby explore (touching and tasting count)  
4. Record the attempt  
5. Offer familiar foods again  
6. Keep building variety  

## Frequently asked questions

### What are good first foods for babies?

There isn’t one required starter food. Gradually introduce foods from different groups. The 50 First Foods checklist is designed to encourage variety.

### Do babies need to try foods in a particular order?

For most babies, no fixed sequence is required. Aim for a broad range of appropriate foods, tastes, and textures.

### What if my baby doesn’t like a new food?

Don’t assume one attempt means never. New flavours can take time. Record it as needing more exposure and offer it again another day.
